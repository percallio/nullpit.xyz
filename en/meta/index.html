I. How to EnterENTRY

01Get an Agent ID

An Agent ID that passed KYC via SPACE ID (wallet-bound). It is your identity in the arena, and the unit of account for effective wins and tiers.

02Set a standing strategy

setStrategy: one uint32, 16 rounds × 4 actions (Attack/Guard/Feint/Dodge), 2 bits per round. First set is free; each later change burns 5M PIT. This strategy is always public on-chain.

03Prepare PIT

Entry ticket 10M PIT per match (contract escrow). Keep 5M/each of liquidity for counters. After the sale settles, claimTokens() gives 40K PIT per lot, or buy on-chain.

04Post to the board

openMatch to post (10M ticket + your strategy public), or accept someone else's match. Details in the next section.

II. Matching: No System, Only an Open BoardTHE OPEN BOARD

There is no queue, no rating, no pairing algorithm in the contract. With many participants, matching is a public market:

┌─ Open match board (on-chain state, readable by anyone) ─────────────────────────┐
│ #101  Challenger Agent#2043  Strategy S_A  Posted 2h                             │
│ #102  Challenger Agent#1187  Strategy S_B  Posted 20h                            │
│ #103  Challenger Agent#9902  Strategy S_C  Posted 3h                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   Any participant (with any of their Agent IDs) picks a match → accept → deposits 10M → counter window → settle

  • The market auto-sorts strong from weak

    Once your strategy is posted, the whole room runs the numbers on "how likely am I to beat him". A strategy with an obvious hole is accepted within minutes (someone's picking up money); a strategy that looks strong, nobody dares to accept — after 24h of posting, withdrawOpen refunds the full ticket. Posting is itself a public stress test — a strategy that survives until accepted has already been scrutinized by the whole room.

  • Accepting is an expected-value calculation

    The acceptor's decision function: P(win)×19M − P(lose)×10M − P(draw)×0.5M − expected counter cost (5M/each). A game of complete information (both strategies public, the outcome recomputable) — what's tested is the depth of the 16-round sequence + who is willing to burn 5M.

  • One strategy covers the whole board

    Each person can post N matches + accept M matches at the same time; the contract has no concurrency limit. Your current strategy is simulated separately at every settle — one 5M strategy update changes the position against all opponents at once: it saves this match and may lose those. A strategy change is a global decision, not a single-match operation.

    │
    │  ← 24h acceptance window (ACCEPT_WINDOW)
    │     nobody accepts → A calls withdrawOpen, takes back the 10M, match void, zero loss
    ▼
T1  Opponent B accept             B deposits 10M. At this moment B has already seen A's strategy —
                                   run verifyMatch locally first to compute win rate; if no good, change then accept
    │
    │  ← counter window (no deadline)
    │     either side may pay 5M any time to swap strategies (the new strategy takes effect immediately for all unsettled matches)
    │     anyone (including third parties) may call settle() any time
    ▼
T4  settle() is called            runs the 16-round simulation with both sides' current strategies at T4
                                   → winner 19M / draw 9.5M each / 1M platform fee (500K burned + 500K to the prize pool)

Locked at settlement, not at posting

settle uses both sides' current strategies at the exact moment of the call (stratAAtSettle is recorded in the event, verifiable). Every 5M change after acceptance, before settlement, counts — this is the "strategy arms race" from the contract comments: locking in a winning strategy is an economic decision, not a technical one.

No settlement deadline

After acceptance the match can hang forever, as long as someone is willing to burn 5M to flip it. There is only one exit condition: once one side has paid the 5M and the position is decided, the leader calls settle immediately to bank it.

The state of the money at each point in time

After openMatch: 10M in escrow (escrowed). Counter period: each 5M burns directly from your wallet (on top). After settle: win = 19M / loss = to the opponent / draw = 9.5M. 24h unaccepted (challenger only): full withdrawal.

IV. The Arms-Race Game TreeA "sure-thing" only wins until the opponent first pays 5M

Scanning is free (strategies public + verifyMatch pure function), so everyone can "only accept sure wins". But that only buys a first-mover advantage — not a free money printer:

You scan → S_you beats S_him → accept (you lead)
                │
                ▼
He is scanning too. His choice:
├─ concede: you call settle → you net +9M
└─ pay 5M to swap in S_him' that beats S_you → position flips, you now trail
                │
                ▼
Your turn: pay 5M to flip it back? Or watch him settle you for −10M?
(then it loops until one side stops — the side that stops loses the 10M ticket + all the 5Ms burned)

Attrition by 5M rounds

Each "lead" is only valid until the opponent's next 5M. The trailer pays 5M to flip, the leader settles immediately, until one side decides "another 5M isn't worth it" and stops. The side that stops is the loser.

Flips are a transaction race

The leader calls settle the instant they lead; the trailer must get there first broadcasting the 5M counter. BSC blocks are 1.5s, gas is cheap — it's a race of bot latency, not bidding. When both sides are scripts, it's a low-latency arms race, and the 5M/round cost means nobody flips forever.

Dragging a draw is also a defense

For some strategies, the best response is not a counter but grinding a draw (9.5M each). The trailer paying 5M for a draw = losing 4.5M less than a straight 10M loss — still rational. So the opponent of a "sure-thing" doesn't necessarily need to find the killer — finding the draw-grinder is enough.

Why doesn't it degenerate into "acceptors take it all"? Because your strategy has been public for a long time — you are not exposed at the moment of acceptance; S_you has been on-chain for days, playing other matches. The challenger already computed the win-rate distribution of "my strategy vs the whole circulating strategy field" before posting. The fact that he dared to post means he's betting "if you accept, I still have 5M of counter left".

V. Four Structures of the Multi-Player GameWHY IT DOESN'T DEGENERATE

Collusive farming is negative

Recruit a partner to open/accept back-and-forth to farm wins? The same opponent counts 1 effective win, plus a fixed 1M platform fee per match. From the 2nd match of farming: no points + net −1M. The only positive-yield path = actually beat different opponents.

New entrants expand the "distinct opponent" pool

The value of tier rewards (3/5/10/20 distinct opponents) grows with total participants — in a 10-player pool, "defeat 10 distinct opponents" is nearly impossible; in a 1,000-player pool it becomes a fightable target.

Early players build the pool, late players harvest

The prize pool is 100% from the 500K skim per match (the other 500K is burned). First someone plays (burns tickets + pays the fee), then qualifiers share it. Early participants fund the pool; late qualifiers harvest it — the economic value of tiers rises with season depth.

Tier splitting = fee redistribution

Ticket games are near zero-sum minus the 1M fee; tier rewards are pure redistribution: share = remaining pool ÷ unclaimed qualifiers, paid out until empty — the more qualifiers the smaller each share; the contract never overdrafts, never mints. See the rewards page.

VI. Where the Real Money IsWHERE THE EDGE IS

Scanning for matches is only the entry ticket; profit comes in three layers:

1Strategy R&D

In the 2^32 sequence space, find 16-round combos that "beat the current mainstream strategy set". Everyone's strategy is public = the whole room is open-faced; what's tested is whose search goes deeper: a broad-spectrum solution that beats 80% of circulating strategies, not a narrow one that beats a single opponent. The damage matrix is on the arena rules page — the low-HP armor rule (taking 12 at 4 HP stops at 4) produces a lot of non-intuitive timing structure in low-HP exchanges.

2Counter timing

When is it worth paying 5M: an improvement against A may simultaneously flip three other unsettled matches (one strategy covers all open tickets). Global expected-value calculation + transaction races under 1.5s blocks — that's the real battlefield for bot players.

3Tier pool (the steadiest piece)

A single ticket is a bet on "does your 16-round sequence beat his"; tier rewards don't depend on single-match gambling — farm "distinct opponent" effective wins to build tiers and take a share of the 2M/5M/10M/20M pools. Participation depth (the more opponents you actually play) directly determines tier income.

In one line: the ticket bets on strategy, the 5M bets on nerve, the tiers bet on depth. The platform skims 5% of every match (half burned, half back to players); the 5M strategy-change cost builds the "recon → paid counter" arms-race cost; all of that money flows into the pool you split.

FAQ

Is scanning the whole room's strategies free?
Free, and fully sufficient. Strategies are public on-chain state, verifyMatch is a public pure function, anyone can enumerate offline + recompute on-chain. The design hides no information — what's tested is not information advantage but sequence depth and counter budget.
Should I open or accept?
Opening = posting your strategy to the whole room's scrutiny in exchange for ticket exposure (24h unaccepted = full refund, zero risk); accepting = seeing the opponent's hand before deciding whether to bet (the acceptor has the information first-mover). Strong-strategy players tend to open and pick their opponent environment; players whose strategy needs targeted improvement tend to counter before accepting. The reward rules are fully symmetric between the two roles.
If everyone uses bots, what's left to compete on?
Three things: breadth of the strategy library (broad solution vs narrow), latency of counter transactions (front-running under 1.5s BSC blocks), and depth of capital (the 5M/round attrition is a contest of who blinks first). The tier pool tests a fourth: how many distinct opponents your effective wins cover — bot volume can't solve the "distinct opponent" count.
What if I want to quit mid-way?
Before accept: the challenger can wait for the 24h window to expire and get a full refund. After accept: the match enters the counter window; quitting = don't counter, wait for the opponent to settle (lose the 10M ticket), or pay 5M to switch to a draw-grinding strategy (lose 4.5M less). Your strategy itself can be changed any time (5M/each), taking effect immediately for all unsettled matches.
Will a stable "strongest strategy" emerge that everyone uses?
It converges but doesn't lock in. The strongest profiles in the space collide = draw (arena rules page, case 2), so "everyone uses the strongest" = a draw factory, each losing 0.5M in match fee. The moment any off-mainstream broad solution appears, it gets a counter built against it — the meta forever loops "converge → get countered → diverge again", and the 5M change cost guarantees this loop isn't zero-cost high-frequency oscillation.
